Play in the real world

Making a game in a real place, without modelling it by hand

We are building automation that turns a captured place (aerial imagery, LiDAR) into game assets: one existing tool per step, each step checked before it hands a file to the next, any tool swappable when something better ships.

Status: a plan, not a demo. One fixed spot (San Francisco's Financial District) runs through the harness, and every handoff is checked.

  • Real: look up public aerial imagery and LiDAR; build a surface with buildings as measured heights; reduce it; drape real aerial imagery on it; assemble one glTF, read back and checked.
  • Not yet: the heights split into 22 separate tall structures, but that is a count: the mesh is still one fused piece, nothing is classified (a crane counts like a tower), and the flight day's sun and shadows are still baked into the colour. The other stages are stubs.

De-lighting

Take the sun and shadows of the capture day out of the colour, so the asset works under any lighting.

Checked for: sunlit and shadowed patches of the same surface match after correction; the result, relit, matches a photo of the place the flow never saw.

Worked through against each tool’s own documentation, 23 September 2026.

Every production tool here assumes the capture avoided hard sun. Unity’s own photogrammetry guide says strong directional shadows are “a situation that must be avoided” because they are “difficult to remove from the generated diffuse albedo texture”. Agisoft says Metashape’s tool does not support strong shadows. Aerial imagery is flown in sunlight, so hard shadows are always in the input. That leaves two ways out at the bottom of this list: measure the shadows from geometry we already have, or skip de-lighting and rebuild the surface from scratch.

Sun-aware de-lighting from known geometry

Our code, public-domain inputs

In
NAIP aerial imagery, a USGS 3DEP LiDAR surface model, the flight date
Out
A cast-shadow mask computed from the geometry, and colour corrected inside it
Driven by
Our code
Idea
Aerial shadows are hard, but they are cast by geometry we already have. Find the sun direction whose shadows over the surface model best match the dark pixels, and the shadow is measured rather than guessed. Then match each shadowed surface to the same surface in sun.
Breaks on
NAIP records the date but not the time, so the sun direction has to be fitted. LiDAR and imagery can be years apart, so trees and new buildings won’t line up.

Rebuild under neutral light, imagery as reference only

Our code, library materials

In
Everything we have for the place: aerial imagery, LiDAR, licensed street photos, and a material library
Out
Geometry and PBR materials authored clean, with no lighting baked in
Driven by
Agents in headless Blender, picking a library material per surface and measuring its colour from the sunlit pixels
Idea
What artists do when the capture can’t be de-lit: use the photos as reference and rebuild by hand. Nothing is baked in, so there is nothing to remove. Colour only has to be measured once per surface, from the parts in sun.
Check
Render the rebuild under the fitted sun and compare it with the original photo.
Breaks on
Fidelity. You get the place’s materials, not its exact pixels, so stains, patches and signage are lost or have to be authored. The hardest path here, and the only one that always ends clean.
